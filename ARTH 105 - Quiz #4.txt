Quiz 4 List of Terms
 
Abhaya mudra: gesture of freedom from fear
 
Ajanta cave 1: Buddhist cave with elaborate wall paintings and sculptures, including Bodhisattvas like Padmapani; exemplifies Gupta-era painting style. Pointed arch motif used stylistically because not structurally integral to a cave. Pillared porch with womb chamber in the back (imitates Sanchi Temple 17). Womb chamber contains Buddha making charamudra (wheel turning/preaching). Paintings of Bodhisattva flank the Buddha.
 
Avalokiteshvara: the Bodhisattva of infinite compassion
 
Ajanta cave 19: Buddhist chaitya (prayer hall) with a stupa and carved façade; shows transition to more developed sculptural decoration. Combination of older stupa architecture and newer temple architecture. Standing Buddha is granting a “boon” (answering prayer). The Buddha’s heam is slashing his shin which creates a border for his body. Snail shell curls. 
 
Anuradhapura: ancient capital city of Sri Lanka; major Buddhist center known for stupas and monastic complexes. City said to have sapling from original Bodhi tree at Bodhgaya. 
 
Bamiyan: massive Buddhist depictions carved into Afghan mountainside from the Gupta period. Overlap of Kushana style (heavier drapery, both shoulders covered) with Gupta era “pan-South Asian” style. Would have had a massive wooden facade. Face is missing. Monks would have come here to pray and study. Caves are also painted → central Asian style as seen by clothing. Destroyed by Taliban in early 2000s. 
 
Bodhgaya Buddha of the Year 64: early Gupta Buddha image with soft modeling, calm expression, and refined spiritual presence.  Shoulders not as broad, face more introspective, moving away from Yaksha style, emphasis on curved lines, cheerfulness is gone, stylistic differences in hair. 
 
dharmachakra mudra: gesture of preaching the law
 
Descent of River Ganges: rock-cut relief cut in granite from the 7th century. Massive 7th century rock-cut relief at Mamallapuram depicting the Ganges’ descent and divine intervention. Story goes that Ganges is a raging body of water being sent onto Earth. Followers of Shiva praying hard for water during drought. On its way down, going too fast so will destroy earth. They now pray for Shiva to help prevent this destruction. Shiva lets the river fall into his hair which tames it and allows it to flow more like a stream. Water is represented by serpents. 
 
Devgarh Temple: 5th century Gupta-era northern temple, Lalitpur district, UP Province. Early Gupta Hindu temple (5th c.) with narrative reliefs; key example of early structural temple architecture. On a raised platform, lots of repetition, mini shrines surround main shrine, doorway contains Yaksha/Yakshi guardians depicted in contraposto framing the entrance to an unembellished dark interior. Tower has collapsed and has been poorly reconstructed. Temple dedicated to lord Vishnu, three “blind windows” that contain reliefs. Vishnu shown sleeping on cosmic serpent, serpent is on the cosmic ocean. Ocean represents primordial waters which becomes associated with Vishnu. Brahma is the creation God → this is the moment before the world comes into existence. Lotus stalk emerges from Vishnu’s naval into the God of creation. Facade is very decorative, very ornamental, inside is empty inner reality that is invisible (atman: Gods presence latently sleeping within us).
 
Elephanta: late-6th century Gupta-era site near Mumbai. Rock-cut cave temple near Mumbai (6th c.); dedicated to Shiva, famous for large sculptural panels. Island became a big temple complex. Reliefs were damaged by Portuguese artillery fire as this site was used for canon practice. 
 
Five rathas: 5 temple-types from the 7th century. Monolithic rock-cut temples at Mamallapuram (7th century) demonstrating different temple architectural forms. Made from local granite. Hypothesized used as an architectural teaching tool because all of the shrines are miniaturized, none are consecrated. 
 
Ganga: the river Ganges
 
garbhagriha: womb-chamber. Windowless room that represents the inner mystery of divinity that is beyond expression.
 
Golvihara, Polonnaruwa: Sri Lankan site with large rock-cut Buddha statues, including reclining Buddha in parinirvana 
 
Govindhnagar Buddha: Kushana-period Buddhas from Mathura; shows regional style and early antropomorphic Buddha form. 
 
Kailash: the mountain which is Shiva’s home. Sacred mountain believed to be Shiva’s abode. Symbolic in Hindu and Buddhist cosmology. 
 
Khandirya Mahadev Temple: at Khajuraho temple complex, 11thcentury, northern India. Temple dedicated to Shiva . Known for complex architecture and erotic sculptures. 
 
linga: aniconic form of Shiva
 
Mamallapuram/Mahabalipuram: South Indian Pallava site (7th c.) known for rock-cut architecture and large reliefs like the Ganges descent. 
 
mandapa: temple pavilion
 
Moonstone: Decorated semi-circular stone at entrances of Sri Lankan temples, symbolizing the cycle of life. 
 
Mukteshvara Temple: in Bhubhaneshvara, Orissa, 10th century, northern India. Known for ornate carvings and refined torana. 
 
Nagarjunakonda Buddha: Southern-style Buddha with elongated form and regional stylistic features. 
 
Nandi: the bull vehicle of Shiva. 
 
Padmapani: the one who holds the lotus. Form of Avalokiteshvara; Bodhisattva holding a locus, symbolizing purity and compassion. 
 
parinirvana: nirvana after death. Release from the cycle of rebirth. 
 
ratha: chariot. Temple form or shrine. 
 
Shore temple: 8th century temple with two sanctuaries, southern India. Temple at Mamallapuram; one of the earliest stone temples in South India. 
 
Shiva Ardhanari: Shiva in the form of half-male and half-female. Form of Shiva as half-male, half-female, symbolizing unity of masculine and feminine principles. 
 
Sigirya: Sri Lankan rock fortress with fresco paintings and royal architecture. 
 
Tara: female Buddhist deity; associated with compassion, protection, and salvation. 
 
Three-headed Shiva: Icon from Elephanta showing Shiva’s three aspects (creation, preservation, destruction)
 
torana: gateway
 
trishula: trident. Shiva’s trident, symbolizing his power and three cosmic functions. 
 
Vajrapani: one who holds the lightning bolt. Symbolizes strength and power.
